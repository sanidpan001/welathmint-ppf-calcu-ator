How PPF Calculator Works? Formula Explained

The PPF interest is calculated using compound interest formula compounded annually. The formula is: F = P[({(1+i)^n}-1)/i] where F is maturity amount, P is annual installment, i is interest rate/100, n is number of years. Our calculator automatically applies this formula. For example, if you invest Rs 1.5 Lakh yearly for 15 years at 7.1%, your total investment is Rs 22.5 Lakh and interest is Rs 18.18 Lakh, total maturity Rs 40.68 Lakh.

Many people search for PPF calculator with monthly investment, but officially PPF allows maximum 12 deposits per year. You can invest monthly Rs 12,500 which equals yearly 1.5 lakh. The interest is calculated on the lowest balance between 5th and last day of month, so always invest before 5th.

PPF Rules You Must Know Before Investing

Minimum investment Rs 500 per year, maximum Rs 1.5 lakh per year. Tenure is 15 years, after that you can extend with or without contribution. You can take loan from 3rd to 6th year up to 25% of balance. Partial withdrawal allowed from 7th year onwards. Account can be opened in any Post Office or authorized banks like SBI, HDFC, ICICI. Only one PPF account per individual allowed. NRI cannot open new PPF account, but existing account can continue till maturity.

For freelancers and business owners, PPF is best because it gives sovereign guarantee. No market risk like mutual funds. Interest rate is decided by government every quarter, but historically it has given 7-8% which beats inflation and FD returns post tax.

PPF vs Mutual Fund vs FD - Which is Better?

If you want guaranteed return and tax saving, PPF is best. FD gives 6-7% but interest is taxable. Mutual fund gives 12-15% but has market risk. Ideal portfolio: 40% PPF for safe, 60% mutual fund for growth. Our PPF calculator shows you safe portion of retirement corpus.
